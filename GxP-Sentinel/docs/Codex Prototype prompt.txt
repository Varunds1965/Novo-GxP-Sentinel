Important Design Decision Before You Build

I would not try to “overcome all AI challenges.” No agent architecture can eliminate them. The defensible engineering objective is to prevent where possible, detect early, contain failures, require human intervention for consequential operations, and produce evidence that controls operated.

That is particularly important now because OWASP's 2026 agentic-AI work identifies risks including goal hijacking, tool misuse, identity/privilege abuse, supply-chain compromise, unexpected code execution, memory poisoning, insecure inter-agent interactions, cascading failures and rogue-agent behavior; OWASP's 2026 incident review also notes that many real-world failures arise from architecture and misconfiguration rather than conventional software CVEs. NIST's AI Risk Management Framework and Generative AI Profile similarly provide a useful risk-management layer around generative systems.

3. CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
======================================================================

Use these principles throughout the implementation:

A. EVIDENCE FIRST
The LLM must never be treated as the system of record.
Enterprise records/documents are evidence.
The agent interprets evidence but cannot invent missing evidence.

B. RULES FIRST, MODEL SECOND
Use deterministic code for measurable control checks whenever possible:
dates, approval state, missing documents, overdue actions, privileged users,
traceability gaps, mandatory fields, status transitions, etc.

Use the LLM for:
- intent understanding;
- reasoning across multiple evidence sources;
- summarisation;
- ambiguity handling;
- recommendations;
- narrative explanations.

Do NOT use an LLM where a deterministic rule is more reliable.

C. ABSTENTION IS A FEATURE
When evidence is missing, stale, contradictory or below confidence threshold,
the system must explicitly say:

"Insufficient evidence to conclude."

Never fill the gap with plausible text.

D. HUMAN ACCOUNTABILITY
No AI agent is the final GxP approver.
Consequential decisions remain human decisions.

E. LEAST PRIVILEGE
Agents receive only the tools/data needed for their assigned task.
An agent must never obtain greater privilege than the current simulated user.

F. SEPARATION OF DUTIES
Separate:
- evidence retrieval;
- domain assessment;
- result verification;
- action authorization;
- action execution.

G. FAIL SAFE
Failure, uncertainty or policy conflict results in:
- read-only mode;
- partial answer;
- escalation;
not uncontrolled execution.

Why This Architecture Is GxP-Defensible

The architecture deliberately pushes measurable compliance decisions into deterministic controls and keeps the generative model in an interpretive/recommendation role. That is consistent with the risk-based lifecycle approach in current EU GMP Annex 11: risk management across the system lifecycle, risk-based validation, traceable user requirements, controlled data processing, audit trails, change management, periodic evaluation, security, incident management, continuity and archiving.

ICH Q9(R1) is also particularly relevant to your A3 agent: the evaluation should be science/evidence based and the degree of effort, formality and documentation should be commensurate with risk. It also explicitly discusses uncertainty and the need to manage subjectivity and bias in quality risk management.

The prompt treats GAMP 5 Second Edition and the 2025 ISPE GAMP Guide: Artificial Intelligence correctly as industry good practice rather than legislation. ISPE describes GAMP 5 as a risk-based approach to fit-for-intended-use GxP computerized systems and its AI guide specifically addresses AI-enabled computerized systems in regulated life-science settings.

One important regulatory nuance: the current published EU GMP Annex 11 remains the 2011 revision. The revised Annex 11 and proposed new Annex 22 for Artificial Intelligence went through consultation in 2025; Annex 22's draft addresses intended use, performance metrics, training/test data, model validation, ongoing monitoring, change control and human review. The prompt therefore treats Annex 22 as future-ready draft guidance, not a currently enforceable final annex.

For U.S. controls, 21 CFR Part 11 remains relevant when electronic records/signatures fall within its scope; FDA continues to emphasize controls such as authorized access, operational/authority checks and electronic-signature requirements. The prompt intentionally does not call the hackathon approval button a Part 11-compliant electronic signature. FDA also issued final Computer Software Assurance guidance in February 2026, but that particular document is scoped to production and quality-management-system software for medical-device manufacturing, so it should not be represented as universally applicable to every drug-manufacturing IT use case.

The EU AI Act also now has transparency requirements applying from 2 August 2026, but provider/deployer obligations depend on the exact system, intended use and legal role. The prototype therefore builds transparency, ownership and disclosure in by design without prematurely declaring the system to be a particular AI Act risk class.

Challenge Check

Stakeholder lens: System Managers get one command centre; QA gets evidence rather than unexplained AI conclusions; Security retains least-privilege enforcement; auditors get provenance; business teams get faster analysis without AI silently becoming the accountable decision-maker.

Risk lens: The largest architectural danger would be letting all seven agents freely call ServiceNow, Vault, identity systems and other enterprise tools. The proposed Action Gateway prevents that and makes the prototype read-mostly and evidence-first.

Counter-argument: A simpler single-agent RAG chatbot could be built much faster. That is true, but it would demonstrate little of the actual hackathon differentiator. This architecture retains the seven-agent experience while using deterministic shared services so you do not create seven independent hallucination engines.

Bias lens: Risk and readiness scoring can easily encode subjective assumptions. The prompt therefore requires transparent configuration, evidence-derived confidence, explicit uncertainty and human adjudication rather than hidden LLM scoring.
